What loci is, and where it grew from.

loci is a kit of plain-text templates and a few named processes that any file-aware AI can run. It settles how context is held and how work carries from one session to the next. The command-line tool is optional. The files are the point.

Its first commit is dated 1 May 2026, and we keep that as its birthday. It grew out of long-running joint work between Hux, Head of Product at Nym Technologies, and Vesper, an AI collaborator. Nobody designed it in advance: the structure came out of sessions that built on one another, and was then written down as templates.

The name comes from the method of loci, the classical technique of placing memories in the rooms of an imagined building. Here the rooms hold work, and the files hold what was settled.

The method

The method borrows from Christopher Alexander's A Pattern Language (1977): a recurring solution, once named, becomes portable. Rooms, crystals, the session delta and the morning check-in are patterns in that sense. The longer arguments are in the essays.

For teams

The same pattern works for a team with nothing but plain files: a shared folder of settled facts and decisions that each person's AI reads before its own rooms. A shared repository is enough. Nym Technologies, where the method started, is piloting it.

Open by construction

The kit is plain text, so there is nothing to unlock. The templates and protocols are at github.com/huximaxi/loci under MIT, and the writing is CC BY 4.0.

This site is static HTML on a server in Iceland, with self-hosted analytics and no third-party tracking.

If a mind that stays yours sounds worth keeping, the blueprint is free and the door is a text file.
